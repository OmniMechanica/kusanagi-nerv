# MLP-017 — Multi-tenancy, authorization and quota fairness

**Project:** KUSANAGI NERV  
**Priority:** P1  
**Schedule:** Day 17  
**Learning/build timebox:** 3–4 h (estimate, not a deadline)  
**Status:** Planned  
**Dependencies:** [MLP-005](MLP-005.md), [MLP-016](MLP-016.md)

## Objective

Keep tenant workloads separated and avoid unfair monopolization of shared resources.

## Implementation steps

- Replace test tenant identity with a small trusted auth context or documented mock JWT boundary.
- Enforce tenant ownership on get/list/cancel/deploy/predict and artifact metadata.
- Implement active execution reservations and GPU quotas as defined in [the data model](../../02-architecture/data-model.md#tenant-reservations-mlp-017). Serialize checks/reservation updates per tenant; pending, unknown and cancelling attempts retain their slot until safe release.
- Document queue fairness policy (e.g. FIFO within tenant plus weighted round-robin across tenants).
- Write isolation, quota and starvation tests.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Tenant A cannot inspect, cancel or deploy Tenant B resources.
- [ ] Quota cap is enforced and reported clearly.
- [ ] Two controllers claiming different jobs with one tenant slot remaining cannot over-admit; local waiting/backoff uses no slot and retries must reserve capacity again.
- [ ] Unknown/unauthenticated access fails after auth activation.
- [ ] Fairness choice and limitations are documented.

## Required evidence

TESTED-CPU for isolation/quota; DESIGN-ONLY for GPU partition isolation not tested.

## What to study in parallel

FastAPI auth lessons ~90–101; Kubernetes RBAC/namespace and platform multi-tenancy guidance.

## Interview drill

How can a tenant bypass quotas through retries? Why is a Ray namespace not necessarily a security boundary?
